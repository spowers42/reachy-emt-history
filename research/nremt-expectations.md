# What NREMT expects from a medical patient history at each Provider Level

Research for issue #4 (map #1). Researched 2026-11-14.

**Question.** For a medical patient, what history elements (chief complaint, SAMPLE, OPQRST, pertinent negatives) and what depth of Field Impression / Differential are expected of EMT, AEMT and Paramedic? How should Student and licensed expectations differ? The output is a per-level table to base the Debrief rubric on.

**Short answer.**

1. **The required history items are the same at every level.** The NREMT medical assessment skill sheets at the EMT level and the advanced level award the same 13 history points: Onset, Provocation, Quality, Radiation, Severity, Time, 2 points for "clarifying questions of associated signs and symptoms related to OPQRST", Allergies, Medications, Past pertinent history, Last oral intake and Events leading to present illness. The chief complaint is scored separately, in the primary survey. [S1, S2]
2. **NREMT scores whether items were covered, not the order they came in.** Each item is a separate point. No critical criterion fails a candidate for asking history questions out of SAMPLE/OPQRST order, or for missing one. The only ordering criterion on the EMT sheet is about doing the secondary exam before dealing with airway, breathing and circulation (ABC) threats. [S1, S2] "Students must go in order" is therefore a teaching choice we would add, not an NREMT rule.
3. **Expected depth increases by level, and only Paramedic is explicitly asked for a differential.** The National EMS Education Standards rate History Taking at foundational breadth and fundamental depth (F,F) for EMT and AEMT, and comprehensive breadth and complex depth (C,C) for Paramedic. Paramedic also adds "Interviewing techniques" and "Therapeutic communication and adaptive interview techniques". For Assessment, only Paramedic is told to "form a field impression. This includes developing a list of differential diagnoses through clinical reasoning." EMT and AEMT are told to "formulate a field diagnosis based upon an actual and/or potential illness or injury." [S3]
4. **AEMT matches EMT for history taking.** Its Assessment competency is "Same as EMT Level", and its History Taking items and depth are identical to EMT's. AEMT differs in diagnostics (blood glucose, etc.) and in treatment. [S3, S2]
5. **NREMT does not define a separate "Student" standard.** NREMT certifies *entry-level* competence at the end of a program. Students are assessed during their program through formative work (feedback, not a grade), then summative evaluations and the Student Minimum Competency (SMC) record. Our Student-vs-licensed split has to be a design decision based on that formative/summative model (see §5). [S5, S6, S7]

## 2. What the skill sheets actually score (history slice)

The two sheets are identical in the part this app cares about. [S1, S2]

| Sheet section | EMT e202/10-16 [S1] | Advanced v2020 (AEMT) [S2] |
|---|---|---|
| Chief complaint | "Determines chief complaint/apparent life-threats" (1), in the Primary Survey | Same (1) |
| History of present illness (OPQRST) | Onset, Provocation, Quality, Radiation, Severity, Time (1 each) + "Clarifying questions of associated signs and symptoms related to OPQRST" (2) = **8** | Same = **8** |
| Past medical history (SAMPLE minus S) | Allergies, Medications, Past pertinent history, Last oral intake, Events leading to present illness (1 each) = **5** | Same = **5** |
| Vital signs | BP, pulse, respiratory rate, respiratory quality = 4 | Same + level of consciousness = 5 |
| Diagnostics | none | "Diagnostics [blood glucose test, pulse oximetry, etc.] as indicated" = 2 |
| Field impression | "States field impression of patient" = **1** | Same = **1** |
| After impression | "Interventions [verbalizes proper interventions/treatment]" = 1 | "Verbalizes treatment plan … calls for appropriate intervention(s)" = 1; "Re-evaluates transport decision" = 1 |
| Total / time | 42 pts | 48 pts, 15-minute limit |

Points to note:
- **The "S" in SAMPLE is not its own line item.** Signs/symptoms are covered by the chief complaint and the 2 "associated signs and symptoms" points. A Debrief should not count it twice.
- **Pertinent negatives are not on either sheet.** They come from the Education Standards, where they are listed for EMT, AEMT (F,F) and Paramedic (C,C) [S3]. In practice, the "clarifying questions of associated signs and symptoms" points are where pertinent negatives would show up.
- **Critical criteria (automatic fail).** On the EMT sheet they cover PPE, scene safety, oxygen and ventilation, airway/breathing/hemorrhage/shock management, the transport decision, the 15-minute limit, "Performs secondary examination before assessing and treating threats to airway, breathing and circulation", dangerous interventions, the report to the arriving unit, "Failure to manage the patient as a competent EMT", and unacceptable affect [S1]. Idaho's version of the advanced sheet lists only "Uses or orders a harmful intervention" and "Exhibits unprofessional behavior" [S2]. **No critical criterion on either sheet concerns which history questions were asked, or in what order.** Missed history items only cost points.
- I did not find the pass mark (minimum points) in any primary source, so it is not given here.

## 3. Per-level depth from the Education Standards and Scope of Practice

| | EMT | AEMT | Paramedic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assessment competency [S3 p.36] | "Applies scene information and patient assessment findings (scene size up, primary and secondary assessment, patient history and reassessment) to guide emergency management." | "Same as EMT Level" | "Integrate scene and patient assessment findings with knowledge of epidemiology and pathophysiology to form a field impression. This includes developing a list of differential diagnoses through clinical reasoning to modify the assessment and formulate a treatment plan." |
| History Taking items (breadth, depth) [S3 p.37] | Investigation of the chief complaint; Mechanism of injury/nature of illness; Associated signs and symptoms; Past medical history; Pertinent negatives — all **(F,F)** | Same items, all **(F,F)** | Same items **(C,C)**, plus "Interviewing techniques (C,C)" and "Therapeutic communication and adaptive interview techniques (C,C)" |
| Clinical Behavior/Judgment – Assessment [S3 p.54] | "Perform a basic history and physical examination to identify acute complaints and monitor changes. Formulate a field diagnosis based upon an actual and/or potential illness or injury." | Same as EMT | "Perform a comprehensive history and physical examination to identify factors affecting the health and health needs of a patient. Relate assessment findings to underlying pathological and physiological changes… Formulate a field diagnosis based on an analysis of comprehensive assessment findings, anatomy, physiology, pathophysiology and epidemiology." |
| Medicine competency [S3 p.38] | "…basic emergency care and transportation based on assessment findings for an acutely ill patient." | "…basic and selected advanced emergency care…" | "…formulate a field impression and implement a treatment/disposition plan for a patient with a medical complaint." |
| Critical thinking [S4 pp.26–29] | "Within a limited set of protocol-driven, clearly defined principles that: Engages in basic risk versus benefit analysis…" | Same as EMT | "Within a set of protocol-driven, clearly defined principles that: Engages in complex risk versus benefit analysis…"; "utilize critical thinking skills to make complex judgments" |
| Certification exam today | Cognitive (2025 spec) + state-run psychomotor that still uses the BLS sheets [S9; see open questions] | Cognitive with **Clinical Judgment 31–35%**; no NREMT psychomotor since 2024-06-30 [S5, S10] | Cognitive with **Clinical Judgment 34–38%**; no NREMT psychomotor since 2024-06-30 [S5, S10] |

**Where "out of order, differential-driven" comes from.** The Paramedic standard asks for "adaptive interview techniques" [S3]. The 2009 wording was "adapt the line of inquiry based on findings and presentation" [S3a]. The Paramedic Assessment competency describes a differential list used "to modify the assessment" [S3]. Together these support letting Paramedics ask hypothesis-driven questions in any order. Nothing in S3 grants AEMT this; it is "Same as EMT Level".

**How deep the Differential should go.**
- EMT and AEMT: give one *field diagnosis / field impression*, supported by the actual or potential findings [S3 p.54; S1, S2 "States field impression"].
- Paramedic: give a field impression *plus* a differential list built through clinical reasoning [S3 p.36]. The retired Paramedic integrated scenario gave its top Integration score for "appropriate field impression, offered a brief summary of **prioritized** differential diagnoses, and identified the appropriate transport destination". The score below that required only an appropriate field impression, patient acuity and destination [S8].
- The current AEMT and Paramedic exams test this as Clinical Judgment: "Recognize cues (assessment & history clues)" → "Analyze cues" → "Define/Prioritize hypothesis (working diagnosis)" [S10, S11]. AEMT is tested on Clinical Judgment too, but the Education Standards do not ask AEMT for a differential *list*. So the reasonable rule for AEMT is a working diagnosis plus awareness of the cues that support it, with a short "what else could it be" credited as a bonus rather than required. This is an inference from S3 and S10, not an NREMT rule.

## 4. Proposed Debrief rubric basis (per Provider Level)

Items marked **[NREMT]** come straight from the sources above. Items marked **[design]** are recommendations that fill gaps NREMT leaves open, including everything Student-specific.

| Provider Level | Required history elements | Order / structure | Field Impression & Differential | "Critical" Debrief flags (out-of-scope items excluded) | Debrief stance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EMT Student** | Chief complaint; O, P, Q, R, S, T; associated S/S (≥2 clarifying questions); A, M, P(ast pertinent hx), L, E **[NREMT S1]**; pertinent negatives **[NREMT S3]** | Teach SAMPLE/OPQRST as a checklist and recommend a consistent order. **Do not fail for order** (NREMT doesn't) **[design]** | State one field impression **[NREMT S1]**; it should match the findings **[NREMT S3 p.54]** | Missed chief complaint; failed to recognize a time-critical presentation (e.g. no urgency on chest pain + diaphoresis) — maps to "Failure to differentiate patient's need for immediate transportation" **[NREMT S1]**; unprofessional affect **[NREMT S1]** | Formative: go item by item ("you asked 11 of 13…"), name each missed item and why it matters **[design]** |
| **EMT** | Same 13 + chief complaint + pertinent negatives **[NREMT S1, S3]** | Coverage is what counts, not sequence; credit efficiency **[design]** | One defensible field impression **[NREMT]** | Same as above | Peer tone: brief, call out only gaps and clinical consequences **[design]** |
| **AEMT Student** | Same as EMT **[NREMT S2, S3 "Same as EMT Level"]**; ask for relevant diagnostics/vitals (e.g. blood glucose with altered mental status, SpO₂ with dyspnea) **[NREMT S2 Diagnostics]** | As for EMT Student **[design]** | Field impression **[NREMT S2]**; optional "what else?" credited, not required **[design, inferred from S3/S10]** | As EMT + failed to ask for an indicated diagnostic (vitals/glucose) when the case depends on it **[design, from S2]** | Formative, item by item **[design]** |
| **AEMT** | Same as AEMT Student | Coverage, not sequence **[design]** | Working diagnosis linked to the history cues that support it ("analyze cues") **[NREMT S10]** | Same as AEMT Student | Peer tone **[design]** |
| **Paramedic Student** | All 13 items + chief complaint + pertinent negatives, at comprehensive depth (C,C), including history that affects health needs (e.g. social or medication-adherence factors) **[NREMT S3]** | May go out of order if questioning follows hypotheses ("adaptive interview techniques") **[NREMT S3]**. The Debrief should still report any items missed **[design]** | Field impression **plus** a differential list built through clinical reasoning **[NREMT S3]**; ideally prioritized and justified **[NREMT S8, S11]**. Expect at least two to three alternatives with the reason each is ruled in or out **[design]** | As AEMT + no differential offered; differential misses a time-critical "can't-miss" diagnosis that the case cues support **[design, from S3/S8]** | Formative: coach the reasoning chain (cue → hypothesis → discriminating question) **[design]** |
| **Paramedic** | Same as Paramedic Student; skipping low-yield items is acceptable when the differential justifies it **[design]** | Hypothesis-driven, any order **[NREMT S3 adaptive interviewing]** | Prioritized differential with discriminating questions and pertinent negatives used to narrow it **[NREMT S3, S8, S11]** | Same as Paramedic Student | Peer / case-review tone; focus on reasoning quality, not checklist completeness **[design]** |

Out of scope for this app (Map #1: history only), even though the sheets score them: PPE, scene size-up, primary survey interventions, physical exam, treatment, reassessment, verbal report. The Debrief rubric should use only the history slice (§2) plus the Field Impression line, and give vitals/diagnostics as Case data on request.

## 5. Student vs licensed: what the sources do and don't say

- NREMT certification measures **entry-level** competency: "the newly certified EMT is able to safely evaluate and manage a scene, as well as assess, treat…" [S9]. A licensed provider is therefore held to at least the full checklist at their level.
- During training, competence is built up over time. NREMT says "Documented skills progression over time via the SMC is stronger evidence of competency than the current ALS psychomotor examination… captured over a longer period, in various settings, and with multiple and differing evaluators" [S5]. Paramedic graduation requires formative and then summative work, including team leads in the capstone field internship [S7]. The AEMT SMC records "progressive proficiency, patient encounters, and team leads" [S6].
- **No source gives a separate, lower history checklist for students.** The defensible split is therefore about **how we give feedback, not what we require**: Students get formative, item-by-item feedback against the full checklist for their level, while licensed providers get the same standard with more credit for efficient, adaptive, differential-driven questioning (Paramedic) or a crisp working diagnosis (EMT/AEMT). **[design]**
- The idea that students must go in order is **our teaching choice**, not NREMT's. The Debrief could *suggest* a structure without penalizing a different one, to stay true to "expectations follow NREMT".

## 6. Uncertainties and gaps

- **EMT sheet version.** e202/10-16 is the newest NREMT EMT medical sheet I could confirm. NREMT says BLS psychomotor exams continue but are set up and approved by each state (from a search snippet of nremt.org's BLS exam page; the page returned 404 when I tried to open it). Some states use modified sheets; for example, Tennessee has a 48-point EMT version, according to search results.
- **Advanced sheet.** Read from Idaho's 2024 republication, not the NREMT original, which has been taken down. The total (48) matches search snippets of the NREMT original, but I could not compare the critical-criteria lists line by line.
- **Which sheet Paramedics used.** I believe the Paramedic psychomotor exam (2017–2024) relied on the Integrated Out-of-Hospital scenario rather than a standalone medical-assessment sheet [S8 context]. I could not open NREMT's paramedic portfolio/exam page to confirm this.
- **Pass thresholds** for the sheets were not found in a primary source.
- **The Paramedic/AEMT test-plan PDFs** (full Clinical Judgment descriptions) returned 404/403. The domain percentages come from the nremt.org Examinations Content page [S10].
- **AEMT differential depth** is an inference (see §3), not an explicit NREMT statement.
