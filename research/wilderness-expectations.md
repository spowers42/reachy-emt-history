# What WFR, WEMT and WP-C curricula expect from a medical patient history

Research for issue #14 (map #1). Researched 2026-11-19. Builds on `research/nremt-expectations.md` (issue #4), which sets the non-wilderness baseline.

**Question.** When Wilderness Certification is set and the Case is a Wilderness Case, what should the Debrief expect of the Trainee? Levels below Paramedic are held to WFR/WEMT expectations and Paramedics to WP-C expectations. For each tier: which history elements are taught or assessed (including wilderness additions), what depth of Field Impression and Differential is expected, and whether evacuation decisions are part of it.

**Short answer.**

1. **No wilderness credential has a scored history checklist like the NREMT sheets.** WFR content is set by a 2016 consensus paper signed by the main US schools: WMA, NOLS, SOLO, Aerie, Wilderness Medicine Training Center, Wilderness Medicine Outfitters and Desert Mountain Medicine [W1]. WEMT is a school-issued add-on to a state or NREMT EMT credential, taught as an "upgrade" or "wilderness module" and assessed by each school's own scenarios and written test [W5, W6, W7]. **WP-C is the Certified Wilderness Paramedic board certification from the International Board of Specialty Certification (IBSC)**, launched 2023-03-08. It is a 135-item computer-based written exam and requires a paramedic license; it has no practical or oral station [P1, P2, P3]. So every per-tier history expectation below is drawn from curriculum content, not from a scoring sheet.
2. **History: SAMPLE with a wilderness "L", plus environment-driven questions.** NOLS and WMA both teach SAMPLE as "Last **Intake/Output**", not "Last oral intake" [W3, W4]. The WMA SOAP form records "Last In / Out" and the urine output time [W4]. The WFR consensus requires "a relevant patient history" and "prevention of medical problems anticipated by the activity and environment" [W1]. The wilderness-specific questions come from the condition guidelines: ascent rate and **sleeping altitude**, prior altitude illness [M1, M3]; **how much the Patient has been drinking**, salt intake, thirst, bloating, length of exertion [M2, M4]; weather, terrain and exposure, and illness in the rest of the group [E1]. OPQRST is near-universal in wilderness texts but I could not find it named in a primary WFR or WEMT standard (see Uncertainties).
3. **Field Impression: a problem list, not a diagnosis, for WFR/WEMT.** WMA and NOLS teach the SOAP "A" as an **Assessment = Problem List**, followed by **Anticipated Problems**, then a Plan [W3, W4, W8]. NOLS describes the assessment as "possible ailments", not a confirmed diagnosis [W8]. No formal Differential is required, but the WFR scope does require telling apart look-alike wilderness conditions, e.g. heat exhaustion/dehydration vs hyponatremia vs heat stroke, and Acute Mountain Sickness vs HACE vs HAPE [W1].
4. **WP-C expects a Differential and environment-aware reasoning.** The WP-C outline tests diagnosis and treatment across general medicine and 14 environmental conditions. It also tests the impact of the environment on medical conditions and medications, drug interactions, and supplements [P2]. The WMS guidelines it lists as study material say care providers "must differentiate" exercise-associated hyponatremia from heat illness and must rule out Acute Mountain Sickness look-alikes [P4, M2, M1]. Expecting a prioritized Differential at WP-C is therefore well grounded, though it is an inference: no WP-C source states it as a rule.
5. **Evacuation decisions are core at every wilderness tier, unlike NREMT.** The WFR consensus lists "conservative decisions on the need for, urgency of and appropriate type of evacuation" as a focus of the course [W1]. NOLS calls evacuation decision-making "an understated difference between urban and wilderness medicine" [W2]. WP-C tests MedEvac logistics, risk stratification, **treat and release**, follow-up after release, anticipatory guidance and preparing for evacuation/flight [P2]. **Nobody standardizes urgency levels.** The most explicit scale I found is the WEMSI WEMT curriculum's three tiers: manage in the field without stopping the trip, end the trip with a non-urgent evacuation, or evacuate immediately [E2].

## 2. Who issues what

| Credential | Issuer | Prerequisite | How it is assessed | Standard behind it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WFR** | Each school (NOLS, WMA, SOLO, Aerie, etc.) issues its own certificate, usually valid 2–3 years | None | School-run written exam and practical skills/scenario assessments [W9, W5] | 2016 consensus SOP [W1]; WMS 1999 topic list [M6] |
| **WEMT** | Each school issues it, on top of a state or NREMT EMT | EMT (or taken together with an EMT course) | School-run simulations and written test [W5, W6] | Curriculum varies by school. Aerie says it follows NREMT [W7]. NAEMSP proposed core content [M5]; no national exam |
| **WP-C** | **IBSC** (International Board of Specialty Certification, the board that also issues FP-C, CCP-C, TP-C) | Current, unrestricted paramedic license; 3 years of wilderness experience recommended | 135-item computer-based exam (110 scored), 2.5 h, Prometric or remote proctor; valid 4 years [P1, P3] | IBSC role delineation study + blueprint [P2]; traces to Millin 2017 [P3, M5] |

So the WFR/WEMT tier is the **NREMT EMT baseline plus a wilderness overlay**: a WEMT holds EMT and adds the wilderness module, and the module's content is essentially the WFR content [W6]. That supports treating WFR and WEMT as one tier, as issue #8 decided.

## 3. History elements

| Element | WFR / WEMT | WP-C | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chief Complaint | Stated first; leads the SOAP report | Same | W8, W4 |
| MOI / HPI | Subjective section: "how an illness developed" | Same | W8 |
| SAMPLE | Taught with **L = Last Intake/Output** (food, fluid, urine) | Assumed (paramedic baseline) | W3, W4; W1a named SAMPLE explicitly, W1 says only "a relevant patient history" |
| OPQRST | Commonly taught but **not named in W1, W3 or W4**; NOLS's SOAP article doesn't use it | Assumed (paramedic baseline) | See Uncertainties |
| Pertinent history: chronic conditions, medications, allergies | Assisting with personal medications is WFR scope | **Explicitly tested:** chronic conditions; "Regularly used medications and supplements (prescription, OTC)"; "Impact of environment on medical conditions and medications"; allergies **and intolerances**; drug–drug interactions | W1; P2 §IV |
| Environment and exposure | "Recognition of environmental conditions that may lead to problems"; prevention "anticipated by the activity and environment" | Environmental Factors domain, 18% | W1, P2 §III |
| **Ascent profile** (altitude reached, **sleeping altitude**, rate of ascent, rest days, prior altitude illness) | Required to recognize Acute Mountain Sickness/HACE/HAPE and "patients who need to stop ascent and acclimatize or descend"; prevention via "acclimatization and ascent profiles" | Altitude-related illnesses tested | W1; M1, M3; P2 |
| **Water/salt intake** (how much drunk, salt/food, thirst, bloating or weight gain, length of exertion, time since exertion up to 24 h) | Required to distinguish heat exhaustion/dehydration, hyponatremia and heat stroke | Dehydration, exercise-induced hyponatremia and heat illness each listed | W1; M2, M4; P2 |
| **Exposure time / cold or heat exposure** (how long out, clothing, wet, wind) | Mild vs severe hypothermia, frostbite, "predisposing environmental and clinical conditions" | Hypothermia, cold injuries | W1; P2 |
| Bites, stings, plants, vectors, water source, group illness | Snakebite, arthropods, camp hygiene and water disinfection; ill group members are part of the social history | Envenomation, adverse flora, vector-borne disease | W1, W1a, E1; P2 |
| Dive profile | WFR **elective** only | Dive-related illness tested | W1a; P2 |
| Tetanus status (wounds) | WEMSI asks for every wilderness wound | — | E1 |
| Team/expedition context | — | "Patient history", "Team medical history", "Team health" under Mission Planning | P2 §I |

What this means for a Case: in a Wilderness Case, a Trainee who asks only the 13 NREMT items will often miss the item that decides the Underlying Condition. For example, an EAH Case and a heat-exhaustion Case can share a Chief Complaint (weak, nauseated, headache) and are separated mainly by **how much the Patient has drunk** and whether they are thirsty [M4].

## 4. Field Impression and Differential

**WFR / WEMT.**
- The output is a **problem list**: WMA's SOAP form is literally "A = Assessment (Problem List)", then "A′ = Anticipated Problems", then "P = Treatment Plan" [W4]. Its worked example lists problems such as "Unstable R upper leg injury", with anticipated problems such as swelling or impaired circulation, sensation and motion [W4]. NOLS: the assessment "convey[s] the problem list" and suggests "possible ailments" [W8].
- WMA's WEMT-level upgrade also teaches a "concise patient problem list" [W5].
- **No Differential list is required**, but discrimination between wilderness look-alikes is in scope. W1 requires recognizing signs and symptoms of heat exhaustion/dehydration, hyponatremia and heat stroke as separate entities with opposite fluid treatments, and of Acute Mountain Sickness vs HACE vs HAPE.
- WEMT sits on top of the EMT standard, so the NREMT EMT rule still applies: one field impression that matches the findings (see `nremt-expectations.md`).
- **Anticipated Problems** have no NREMT equivalent. They fit a long evacuation: what this Patient may develop over the next hours.

**WP-C.**
- The paramedic baseline already requires a Differential (National EMS Education Standards; see `nremt-expectations.md`).
- The WP-C blueprint widens it: "Diagnostics and treatment" across general medicine, 14 environmental conditions, and the effect of the environment on chronic conditions and medications [P2].
- The WMS guidelines it recommends [P4] are explicitly differential-driven. EAH must be told apart from heat exhaustion and exertional heat stroke, and "failure in this regard is a recognized cause of worsened morbidity and mortality" [M2]. Before treating HAPE, consider asthma, pneumonia, PE, pneumothorax, MI and viral infection [M3]. Altitude illness look-alikes include dehydration, exhaustion, hypoglycemia, hypothermia, hyponatremia and CO poisoning (from secondary summaries of M1; not confirmed in the guideline text).
- No WP-C document says "state a prioritized differential". This is an inference from the paramedic baseline plus P2 and P4.

## 5. Evacuation decisions

| | WFR / WEMT | WP-C |
|---|---|---|
| Is it assessed? | **Yes, central.** "Conservative decisions on the need for, urgency of and appropriate type of evacuation" [W1]; "Decide on need and urgency of evacuation" is a core assessment skill [W1]. NOLS: "I need to decide if the patient needs to see the doctor, and if so, how urgently" [W2]. Evacuation planning appears in WMA upgrade scenarios [W5] | **Yes, broader.** Transportation/MedEvac considerations and healthcare facility evaluation; risk stratification; **treat and release** and follow-up after release; anticipatory guidance; "Package and Prepare for Evacuation/Flight"; helicopter and rescue interface [P2] |
| Red-flag triggers | W1 defers detailed evacuation guidelines to "elsewhere in the literature". The 2010 draft gave them per condition, e.g. altitude illness with dyspnea at rest (HAPE) or ataxia/mental status change (HACE); Acute Mountain Sickness that is not acclimatizing; abdominal pain worsening over 12 h, fever, blood, pregnancy signs [W1a] | Not specified; implied by the WMS guidelines (e.g. descend for severe Acute Mountain Sickness, HACE, HAPE [M3]) |
| Urgency scale | **Not standardized.** WEMSI WEMT: manage in the field / end task, non-urgent evacuation / immediate evacuation [E2]. NOLS case studies use "rapid evacuation" | Not standardized |
| Mode | "Appropriate type of evacuation": walk-out, carry-out, or call SAR/EMS [W1] | Mode selection including air; landing zones [P2] |

## 6. Proposed Debrief rubric basis (Wilderness Certification set, Wilderness Case only)

**[src]** = directly supported by the sources above. **[design]** = our choice to fill a gap.

| | **WFR/WEMT tier** (EMT Student, EMT, AEMT Student, AEMT with Wilderness Certification) | **WP-C tier** (Paramedic Student, Paramedic with Wilderness Certification) |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | The Provider Level's NREMT rubric (13 history points, Chief Complaint, field impression) still applies **[design, per #8]** | The Paramedic NREMT rubric (incl. Differential) still applies **[design, per #8]** |
| SAMPLE "L" | Credit **Last intake AND output** (fluids, food, urine). Missing output is a minor miss **[src W3, W4]** | Same **[src]** |
| Environment-triggered history (required only when the Case's Underlying Condition or setting makes it relevant) | Altitude Case: altitude now, **sleeping altitude**, rate of ascent, prior altitude illness. Heat/exertion Case: **fluid volume, salt/food, thirst, duration of exertion**, urine. Cold Case: **exposure time**, wet/wind/clothing. Bite/sting/plant: what, when, where. GI/fever: water source, ill group members **[src W1, M1–M4, E1; trigger mapping is design]** | Same, plus: medications/supplements and **how the environment changes them** (heat and diuretics or beta-blockers, altitude and sleep aids), intolerances, drug interactions; dive profile if water-based **[src P2]** |
| Field Impression | A **problem list**: the main problem(s) the Trainee suspects, stated as working impressions (e.g. "possible HAPE", "dehydration, maybe hyponatremia") **[src W4, W8]**. Credit **Anticipated Problems** as a bonus **[design]** | A field impression **plus a prioritized Differential** that includes the relevant wilderness look-alikes (EAH vs heat exhaustion vs heat stroke; Acute Mountain Sickness vs dehydration, hypoglycemia, CO; HAPE vs pneumonia, PE) **[inference from P2, M1–M3]** |
| Discriminating questions | Flag if the Trainee settled on one of a known look-alike pair without asking the question that separates them (e.g. fluid intake before calling it dehydration) **[src W1 scope; flag is design]** | Required: the Debrief names the discriminators asked or missed **[design]** |
| Evacuation decision | **Required.** State whether to evacuate and how urgently, on a 3-level scale: *stay and monitor* / *non-urgent evacuation* / *urgent (immediate) evacuation*, with a reason tied to a red flag **[src W1, W2; scale from E2]** | **Required,** plus *mode* (walk, carry, air) and when *treat and release* is acceptable, with follow-up/anticipatory guidance **[src P2]** |
| Critical flags | Missing a red flag that demands urgent evacuation (e.g. ataxia or confusion at altitude, dyspnea at rest at altitude, altered mental status after prolonged exertion) **[src W1a, M3, M4]**; advising fluids for a Patient whose history points to hyponatremia **[src M2/M4]** | Same, plus a Differential that omits a life-threatening look-alike the history pointed to **[design]** |
| Not expected | No Differential list; no medication decisions beyond assisting with the Patient's own **[src W1]** | — |

Implementation note for Cases: each Wilderness Case should carry the environment facts above (ascent profile, fluid/salt intake, exposure time, etc.) as answerable history, plus a "correct" evacuation urgency for the Debrief to judge against. That is a change to the Case shape. **[design]**

## 7. Uncertainties and open questions

1. **Millin 2017 [M5] was not read beyond its abstract.** It is the NAEMSP Delphi core content that WP-C traces to, with separate WEMT and Wilderness Paramedic content. It may state history, assessment or evacuation expectations per level that would sharpen or contradict the table above. Worth getting through a library.
2. **OPQRST is not named in any primary WFR/WEMT standard I could read.** W1 (2016) says only "a relevant patient history"; W1a (2010 draft) and WMA's form name SAMPLE. OPQRST is in common use in wilderness textbooks, and WEMTs hold EMT, so keeping the NREMT OPQRST points is reasonable. But "WFR curricula require OPQRST" is unverified.
3. **WEMT has no single standard.** It varies by school; Aerie claims NREMT alignment from a document whose authorship I could not confirm [W7]. WMA's upgrade page was read only through a search index [W5].
4. **WP-C is written-only.** There is no practical station, so nothing directly says what a WP-C holder's spoken history or Differential should look like. The WP-C column is inferred from the blueprint topics and the WMS guidelines IBSC recommends.
5. **The WP-C blueprint is dated 2022.** The "Updated" file IBSC serves now has the same content; I did not see a newer revision or read the candidate handbook.
6. **WMS guidelines were read through abstracts and AAFP summaries.** The altitude summary [M3] covers the 2019 update, not 2024. The altitude-illness look-alike list (CO, dehydration, hypoglycemia, etc.) came from secondary summaries.
7. **The WMS 1999 WFR topic list [M6] was not read.** The WMS is not a signatory of the 2016 consensus; Wikipedia calls the 2016 authors the "Wilderness Medicine Educators Collaborative", which I could not confirm from a primary source.
8. **Evacuation urgency has no standard vocabulary.** The 3-level scale is borrowed from a 1990s WEMSI draft. Open question for the map: do we adopt it, or let the Debrief accept free-text urgency and judge it?
9. **Open design question:** should Anticipated Problems be credited at the WFR/WEMT tier, or only mentioned? Nothing in NREMT corresponds to it, but it is on every WMA SOAP note.
10. **Open design question:** should environment-triggered history items be scored only when relevant to the Underlying Condition, or always for Wilderness Cases (e.g. always ask about fluid intake)? The sources teach them as condition-driven, so "only when relevant" is the default above.
